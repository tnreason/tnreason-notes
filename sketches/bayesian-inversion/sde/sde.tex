\section{Stochastic Differential Equations}

We generalize to stochastic differential equations by considering probability distributions over parameters.

\begin{example}[Eulers method with random parameters]
    Consider an ODE discretized to $\shortcatvariables$ with parameters enumerated by $\thirdcatvariableof{[\catorder-1]}$.
    Eulers method gives the next function value $\catindexof{\catenumerator+1}$ based on the current $\catindexof{\catenumerator}$ and parameter $\thirdcatvariableof{\catenumerator}$.
    We denote $\formulaof{\catenumerator}$ as function computing these Euler steps.
    When we have a distribution $\probtensor$ of the parameters, then the Euler approximated solution of the SDE is the contraction:
    \stantikzpic{
        \drawhblock{0}{0}{3}{$\bencodingof{\formulaof{0}}$}
        \drawtdwire{-2}{-1}{$\catvariableof{0}$}
        \drawddwire{0}{1}{$\thirdcatvariableof{0}$}
        \drawddwire{2}{-3}{$\catvariableof{1}$}

        \drawvariabledot{3.5}{-5}
        \draw[-<-] (3.5,-5) to [bend right= -10] (2,-3);
        \draw[->-] (3.5,-5) to [bend right= 10] (5,-3);
        \drawddwire{3.5}{-7}{$\catvariableof{1}$}

        \drawhblock{7}{0}{3}{$\bencodingof{\formulaof{1}}$}
        \drawtdwire{5}{-1}{$\catvariableof{1}$}
        \drawddwire{7}{1}{$\thirdcatvariableof{1}$}
        \drawddwire{9}{-3}{$\catvariableof{2}$}

        \drawvariabledot{10.5}{-5}
